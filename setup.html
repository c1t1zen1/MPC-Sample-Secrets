Charging rules official

  • Power and charge over USB-C: wall adapter, power bank, or a computer's USB-C host port. No adapter in the box.
  • The charge icon lights only while charging. It goes dark when unplugged or full.
  • Gotcha: depending on the cable and source, it may only charge while powered off. Use the included cable and at least 5 V / 2 A.
  • At full charge on external power it runs from the source and only trickles when off.

Battery LED official

■ green100–30%
■ amber29–15%
■ red14–5%
▣ blinking red<5%: connect power now

Runtime: conflict the manual says ~5 h of continuous playback, the FAQ says "up to 6 hours". Speaker volume and backlight use change it. Teardown notes report a TI bq fuel-gauge routine in the panel MCU firmware.

Speaker logicThe built-in speaker switches off when anything is plugged into PHONES or AUDIO OUT, and while the internal Mic is the source with monitoring On/Auto (feedback guard). Silent unit? Check for a forgotten cable. Some 1.3.0 updaters also reported a muted speaker straight after updating.
2.2

Firmware & update mode

The unit runs a web server over USB. That's how official updates work, and it's the same door the hackers knock on (see 04).

Browser updater official

  1. Power on, connect USB-C to a computer (or USB-C iPhone/iPad).
  2. Open http://mpc-sample.local. If that fails, power-cycle while connected, then try http://192.168.155.1.
  3. The page scans your version. Click update and stay connected; it reboots itself.
  4. Got a file already? Manual Firmware Upload on the same page. The inMusic Software Center also works.

Sources

Update mode (desktop updater) official

  1. Power off.
  2. Hold and press POWER.
  3. Connect USB, run the MPC Sample Updater from the downloads page.

Required on Windows 10: the browser updater isn't supported there. Also your fallback when the web page won't come up.

iOS & Lightning official

USB-C iPhones/iPads connect directly with the included cable. Lightning devices need Apple's Lightning to USB 3 Camera Adapter with a 20 W USB-C charger on its pass-through, plus a full-data USB-A→USB-C cable to the MPC.

Linux & stubborn macOS community

The MPC shows up as a USB network adapter (NCM) at 192.168.155.1/28 with its own DHCP. .local needs mDNS, so use the IP. Linux users got it working by letting the browser talk only to the MPC's interface (e.g. usb0) in a private window; some Mac users needed the IP too. 

# see the link the MPC creates, then open the updater
ip addr | grep -A2 192.168.155
xdg-open http://192.168.155.1/

The five personalities of USB-C

One port, five jobs. Some can't run at the same time, which explains most "my computer can't see it" moments.

ModeHow to get itDetailsBadge
PowerPlug inBus power, wall or power bank. 5 V 2 A recommended.official
Audio I/OInput Config / Sample Record source = USB, USB L or USB RStereo in from Mac/Win/iOS/Android, so you can sample a phone app or DAW straight in. Reverse engineers see a UAC2 gadget running 2 ch at 44.1 kHz. Since 1.2, sample previews also play over USB audio. official
MIDI → MIDI Port = USBClass-compliant port named MPC Sample (USB VID 0x09E8, PID 0x205C). Silent until you switch the port to USB. The device echoes incoming MIDI back out (soft-thru). verified
NetworkAutomatic when powered on and connectedUSB NCM Ethernet: MPC at 192.168.155.1/28 with DHCP; web UI at mpc-sample.local (updater; auth disabled in config). verified
Disk → SD Card AccessThe microSD mounts on the computer as a drive. While it's mounted, other modes are locked and the USB-MIDI endpoints disappear. Eject on the computer first, then B1. verified
Not a controller for MPC 3Akai says the MPC Sample does not work as a controller for the MPC 3 desktop software. It is still an ordinary class-compliant MIDI device, so any DAW or Web MIDI page can play its pads (and its pads can play things).

TRS Type A

The 3.5 mm MIDI jacks are Type A (spelled out from manual RevC on). Use Type-A TRS↔DIN adapters; a Type-B adapter (Arturia/Novation style) won't work. conflict the manual says adapters aren't included, while the FAQ talks about "the included adapter". Check your box.

Follow a DAW

  1. MIDI Port → USB
  2. MIDI Sync In → MIDI Clock
  3. DAW: send clock to "MPC Sample"
  4. Press PLAY in the DAW

Lead a drum machine

  1. MIDI Port → External (TRS-A → DIN)
  2. MIDI Sync Out → MIDI Clock
  3. MIDI Out Channel = the machine's channel
  4. PLAY on the MPC
2.5

Sync Out & PPQN

SYNC OUT is a 3.5 mm TS jack with 5 V clock pulses for modular gear, Volcas and Pocket Operators. Clock only: there's no separate run/stop line.

Pulses per quarter = Base × Division, per the manual's definition.

TargetPPQNBase × Div
Quarter-note clock / clock divider11 × 1
Korg Volca, Teenage Engineering PO (1 pulse per 8th)22 × 1
Eurorack 16th-note sequencers44 × 1
24 PPQN clock inputs248 × 3 · 1 × 24
48 PPQN clock inputs482 × 24

Target rates are common conventions for that gear, not Akai statements; check your device's manual. verify

Getting sounds in & out

8 GBinternal (~2 GB factory)
2 GBRAM
128 / 128samples / seqs per project
20 minmax per sample
24-bit44.1 kHz recording
32-bit floatengine

Four ways in

  1. Card reader: copy WAVs to the microSD on your computer. The MPC creates MPC-Sample/Samples on the card. Browse with SAMPLE SELECT → B2 (External), though you can navigate anywhere on the card.
  2. SD Card Access (fw 1.2+): → SD Card Access → drag files over USB → eject → B1.
  3. Record over USB audio from a phone, tablet or DAW: source = USB.
  4. Phone workflow: Andy Mac's tip is to grab samples on a phone (e.g. Splice) and move them onto the SD card.

Formats it eats official

Import: .wav .aif/.aiff .mp3 .flac .ogg, plus vintage Akai .snd (MPC2000-era) and .s1s/.s3s (S1000/S3000-era). Files at 16/24-bit and 44.1/48/96 kHz load; the engine runs at 44.1 kHz.

Save a sample: → name → B3 Do It.

Card format: not specified by Akai. Community tools use exFAT cards without trouble. community

Projects are an .xpj file plus a matching [ProjectData] folder. Move both or the project won't reopen.

microSD layout (as created by the MPC)
/  (microSD root)
├── MPC-Sample/
│   ├── Samples/        ← default browse target for SAMPLE SELECT › B2 (a User/ subfolder shows up in the wild)
│   └── Projects/       ← projects travel as a pair: MyBeat.xpj + MyBeat_[ProjectData]/
└── anything/you/like/  ← the browser can navigate anywhere on the card

MPC-Sample/Samples is documented ; the Projects and Samples/User folders were observed by community tool builders .

2.7

MPC 3.8 round trip

What works now official

  • MPC Sample projects open on MPC hardware and MPC desktop running 3.8+ (free update, released 30 Apr 2026).
  • 3.8 can also export tracks back to the MPC Sample.
  • 3.8 brings MPC Sample effects to the big MPCs (Color compressor, tape & vinyl emulators, vintage emulation and more).
  • Don't push full MPC 3 projects onto the Sample. Akai says not to.

Tricks & history

  • Launch manual v1.0 promised MPC 3 loading; RevB walked it back to "not currently supported, coming in a future update"; 3.8 delivered it.
  • Can't see the project data in the MPC 3 browser? Forum users point to the browser's gear menu to unhide [ProjectData] folders. community
  • Need Ableton? MPC2Live converts MPC projects to Live Sets.
  • A popular video claims you can get MPC Expansion content onto the Sample by way of MPC 3. unverified

Factory resets

Reset Factory Settings

Settings back to default, then restart. Your content stays. B3 Yes · B1 No.

Reset Factory Data

Wipes all internal user content, including the current unsaved project, and restores the factory library. No undo. Copy projects to the SD card first.
